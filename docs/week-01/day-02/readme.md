# Day 02 - Client requirements

## 10 steps to writing good software requirments
01. Identify stakeholders and gather input
02. Define the problem
03. Outline high-level features and functionalities
04. Create specific, auctionable requirements
05. Prioritize requirements
06. Review requirements with stakeholder
07. Refine and clarify based on feedback
08. Document assumptions and constraints 
09. Include relevant diagram or mockups
10. Establish a process for managing requirement changes

Example website: https://www.nasa.gov/

### Navigation and Search
1. The website shall provide a main navigation menu covering missions, humans in space, Earth, The solar system, The universe, Science, Aeronautsics, Technology, Learning Resources, About NASA, News and Events, and Multimedia.

2. Each main menu item shall expand to show related sub-pages (Missions -> Artemis, ISS, Hubble, Webb, Upcoming Launches)

3. The website shall provide a site-wide search function with suggested popular searches (Artemis, ISS, Webb)

4. The website shall provide an A-Z listing of all topics and an A-Z listing of all missions.

### News and conten
1. The homepage shall display featured news articles with title, image, estimated reading time, and how long ago it was published.

2. The homepage shall show topic-based "Highlights" sections (Earth, Solar System, Universe, Aeronautics, Technology, Learning) with the latest three articles each.

3. The website shall provide a "Recently Published" page listing all new content.

4. Content types shall include news releases, articles, and blogs, each labeled by type.

### Live missions and events
1. The homepage shall feature a hero section highlighting a current mission event with a video clip and summary text.
2. The website shall display the active ISS missio and the next upcoming cargo mission, with links to their pages.
3. The website shall provide a schedule of upcoming launches and landings.
4. The website shall promote special public events with dates, location, description, and a "Learn More" link.

### Multimedia
1. The homepage shall display an "Image of the Day" with a caption, a link to the full image page, and a link to the image archive.
2. The website shall provide image galleries grouped by observatory showing the number of images in each.
3. The website shall link to NASA + streaming, NASA Live, podcasts, e-books, 3D resources, and NASA apps.

### Audience-specific sections
1. The website shall provide learning resources separated by audience: students, educators, and professionals.
2. The website shall provide careers and internship information.
3. The website shall provide a "Doing Business with NASA" section for companies and contractors.
4. The website shall provide media resources and media contacts for journalists.
5. The website shall provide a spanish-languag section.

### Engagement
1. Users shall be able to subscribe to an email newsletter, with a clear statement that their email won't be shared and a link to the privacy policy.
2. The website shall link to NASA's official social media accounts.
3. Each page shall include a "Was this page helpful?" Yes/No feedback form.

### Legal, compliance and transparency
1. The footer shall include links to Privacy Policy, FOIA, No FEAR Act, Office of the Inspector General, Budget and Annual Reposts, Accessibility, and Contact NASA.
2. Each page shall show the last updated date, page editor, and responsible NASA official.

### Non-functional requirements
1. The website shall be fully responsive on desktop, tablet, and mobile.
2. the website shall meet accessibility standards including alt text for all images.
3. Pages shall load quickly even with heavy images and video.
4. The website shall support high traffic spikes during major events such as launches.
